Why Won’t My Live Photo Convert to GIF on iPhone?

Start with a playable video, rather than the still-image part of a Live Photo.

A Live Photo and a video file are different inputs

A Live Photo combines a still image with a short moving moment. Selecting or sharing it into a website can provide only a HEIC or JPEG image, depending on the route you use. A still image does not contain the frames needed for an animated GIF. That does not mean iPhone cannot make GIFs; it means this converter needs a video that the browser can decode.

This website converts video to GIF. Its Live Photo output option goes in the other direction: video → a desktop Live Photo package. It does not import a Live Photo package, LIVP file or PVT directory to make a GIF, and choosing that output option will not solve an input problem.

Export the motion first

  1. Open the original Live Photo in Apple Photos.
  2. Open the More menu and choose Save as Video, when available. Apple documents this workflow; menu placement can vary with iOS and the photo’s state.
  3. In this site’s video chooser, select the resulting video. Make sure the preview plays and has a non-zero duration, then choose GIF.

See Apple’s instructions for turning a Live Photo into a video. The original Live Photo remains in Photos. If the command is missing, check that you opened the original Live Photo and consult Apple’s guidance for your iOS version.

If conversion still fails

  • Only a still picture appears: confirm that you selected the exported video rather than the HEIC or JPEG. A different filename is not proof of a different media type.
  • The browser cannot play it: container and codec support vary. Try exporting a browser-playable video through the device’s normal tools. Renaming the extension does not change its encoding.
  • A resource limit appears: keep the file under 200 MB, trim a shorter moment, choose a smaller size and reduce FPS. A 450-frame output budget also applies; slow motion and Ping-pong consume more frames.
  • Nothing should upload: the conversion stays local, but a phone still needs enough memory and time to decode and encode. Keep the page open until the result is ready.

Safari on iPhone is a GIF-use target, not a promise that every video codec, background state or device will complete every conversion. This article does not claim that iOS Live Photo import or direct mobile Live Photo export is supported by the website. For the normal GIF workflow, see How to use.
